# International telework (digital nomad)

> Consolidated BOE text is informative; the official gazette publication is the only legally binding source.

Generated from `data/requirements/digital-nomad.json`. Ley 14/2013 arts. 74 bis–quinquies, as inserted by Ley 28/2022.

### Who this residence is

Residence for international telework is a third-country national authorised to remain in Spain to carry out a labour or professional activity at a distance for companies established outside Spain, using exclusively computer, telematic and telecommunication means.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 bis.1; BOE-A-2022-21739 art. disposición final quinta.9

### Union citizens are outside this law

Section 2 of Title V of Ley 14/2013 does not apply to EU citizens or to foreigners who enjoy equivalent free-movement rights under EU agreements.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 61.2

### Employed vs professional: the 20% ceiling

If the activity is labour (employed), the holder may work only for companies established outside Spain. If the activity is professional, work for a company in Spain is allowed only if that work is not more than 20% of total professional activity.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 bis.1

### Qualified professionals

The visa or authorisation may be requested by qualified professionals who prove they are graduates or postgraduates of universities of recognised prestige, vocational training, or business schools of recognised prestige, or who have a minimum of three years’ professional experience.

**In the file:** Degree/postgraduate diploma from a university, VET centre or business school of recognised prestige — or evidence of at least three years’ professional experience in functions analogous to the remote role.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 bis.2

### The foreign company must have one year of real activity

Prove real and continuous activity for at least one year of the company or group with which the worker has a labour or professional relationship.

**In the file:** Certificate from the companies register (or equivalent body) showing date of incorporation (not less than one year) and type of activity.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 ter.a

### The work can actually be done remotely

File evidence that the labour or professional relationship can be performed remotely.

**In the file:** Written proof that the role is performed exclusively by computer, telematic and telecommunication means.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 ter.b

### Employed: three months’ relationship plus remote permission

For a labour relationship, prove that it has existed with the non-Spanish company for at least the three months before filing, and that the company permits remote work.

**In the file:** Employer certificate: seniority (minimum three months) and express permission to work remotely from Spain, stating profile, salary, and terms.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 ter.c

### Professional: three months’ mercantile relationship plus terms

For a professional relationship, prove a mercantile relationship with one or more companies not located in Spain for at least the last three months, and the terms on which the remote professional activity will be exercised.

**In the file:** Mercantile contracts / invoices covering at least three months, plus a document setting the remote terms and conditions.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 ter.d

### General Ley 14/2013 requirements also apply

In addition to art. 74 ter, the general requirements of art. 62 apply: not irregular in Spain; over 18; criminal record; not listed as inadmissible; health insurance; sufficient resources for the holder and family; payment of the fee.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3; BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 ter

### Eighteen or older

The applicant must be over 18.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3.b

### Not in an irregular situation in Spain

The applicant must not be in an irregular situation in Spanish territory.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3.a

### Criminal record: two-year certificates plus five-year sworn declaration

No criminal record in Spain or in the countries of residence during the last two years, for offences under Spanish law. In addition, file a sworn declaration of no criminal record covering the last five years. The joint instruction restates this packet: certificates for the last two years of residence, plus the five-year declaration. The official FAQ states the two-year certificates plus five-year declaration are not required where the person already holds a Spanish residence or stay authorisation of more than six months.

**In the file:** Criminal-record certificates from each country of residence in the last two years (apostilled or legalised, sworn translation) + sworn declaration covering five years.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3.c; process:instruccion-conjunta-teletrabajo; process:dn-faq-en

### Not listed as inadmissible

The applicant must not appear as inadmissible (rechazable) in the territory of countries with which Spain has signed an agreement to that effect.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3.d

### Public or authorised private sickness insurance

Art. 62.3.e requires public insurance or private sickness insurance contracted with an insurer authorised to operate in Spain. The official UGE FAQ adds operational limits: travel insurance is not valid; policies limited to reimbursement of medical expenses are not valid; policies with waiting periods or co-payments are not valid. Public cover through registration in Spanish Social Security also satisfies the requirement.

**In the file:** Certificate of public cover (SS registration / bilateral coverage certificate) or a private full-cover policy with an insurer authorised in Spain — not travel, not reimbursement-only, no waiting periods, no co-payments.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3.e; process:dn-faq-es

### Resources: 200% SMI holder · 75% first family member · 25% each additional

Art. 62.3.f requires sufficient resources for the holder and family. The joint instruction (applying that article) sets the amounts: holder, a monthly amount representing 200% of SMI; family unit of two (holder + first family member), at least 75% of SMI for that first member; 25% of SMI for each additional member. Proof may be any means admitted in law, including the employment contract, firm job offer, or mercantile contract. Ownership, lawfulness and availability of funds used as proof must be accredited. This is a formula. Do not treat this year’s euro arithmetic as the statute — see conflict smi-monthly-conversion.

**Formula:** `holder = 200% × SMI (monthly); first family member = 75% × SMI; each additional family member = 25% × SMI`

**In the file:** Contract, firm offer or mercantile contract showing the remote income, plus any bank evidence of ownership, lawfulness and availability of the funds relied on.

> **Conflict — not settled.** 200% SMI is the rule. The euro conversion is not.

Ley 14/2013 art. 62.3.f requires sufficient resources. The joint instruction of the Ministries of Inclusion and Foreign Affairs fixes the amount as 200% of SMI per month for the holder, 75% of SMI for the first family member, and 25% of SMI for each additional member. RD 126/2026 publishes SMI as 1,221 €/month (14 payments) and an annual comparison floor of 17,094 €. The instruction does not say whether ‘mensualmente el 200% del SMI’ uses the gazette monthly figure or the annual figure divided by twelve. This codex therefore publishes the legal formula only, and shows both arithmetic illustrations as illustrations — never as the statute.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.3.f; process:instruccion-conjunta-teletrabajo; process:one-dn-visa

### Social Security: coverage certificate or Spanish registration commitment

Plataforma ONE and the joint-instruction document list require either a Social Security coverage certificate (where a bilateral agreement applies) or registration / a request to register the foreign company in the Spanish Social Security system, with a commitment to complete registration once the authorisation is granted and before work starts. Art. 76.5 allows the passport to suffice for SS registration during the first six months if the foreigner does not yet hold an NIE.

**In the file:** Bilateral SS coverage certificate, or evidence of the request to register the foreign employer in Spanish Social Security plus a responsible declaration to complete alta before activity starts.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 76.5; process:one-dn-residence; process:mae-mexico-dn-visa

### From abroad: consular visa, not UGE-CE

Foreigners who are not residents in Spain and intend to reside in Spain to telework for a company not located in Spain must apply for the international-telework visa. The official FAQ states it is not possible to apply from abroad directly for the residence authorisation. Apply at the Spanish consular office competent for the country of residence.

> **Conflict — not settled.** Some consular pages add local filing rules that are not national eligibility law

The national file is Ley 14/2013 arts. 74 bis–quater plus the joint instruction. Individual consulates publish extra local conditions (for example a pre-visa NIE, a cash fee in local currency, or a longer passport validity). Those pages are official for that post’s filing window. They are not a national amendment of arts. 74 bis–quater. This codex does not treat them as settled Spanish eligibility law.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 quater.1; process:dn-faq-en; process:one-dn-visa

### Visa validity: maximum one year

The international-telework visa has a maximum validity of one year, unless the work period is shorter, in which case the visa has the same validity. The visa is in itself a sufficient title to reside and work remotely in Spain during its validity.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 quater.1–2

### Sixty days before the visa expires: apply for the three-year residence

Within sixty calendar days before the visa expires, holders who wish to continue residing in Spain may apply for the international-teleworker residence authorisation, provided the conditions that generated the right remain.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 quater.3

### Already lawfully in Spain: UGE-CE residence via Plataforma ONE / sede

Foreigners who are in Spain in a regular situation, or who entered on the telework visa, may apply for a residence authorisation to telework for a company located abroad, valid throughout Spain. File electronically with the UGE-CE. Plataforma ONE describes the channel: MI-T (and MI-F for family), pay tasa 038 and obtain an NRC, then submit on the UGE-CE sede with a digital certificate or Cl@ve.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 quinquies.1; BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 76.1; process:one-dn-residence

### Residence validity: maximum three years

The residence authorisation has a maximum validity of three years unless requested for a shorter work period. Plataforma ONE restates: three years, or less if the contract is shorter.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 quinquies.2; process:one-dn-residence

### Renewal: two-year periods, conditions maintained

Holders may request renewal for two-year periods provided the conditions that generated the right are maintained. Art. 76.3: renewals are electronic; filing extends validity until the decision, including if filed within ninety days after expiry (without prejudice to a possible sanction file).

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 quinquies.3; BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 76.3

### UGE-CE decides in twenty days; silence is positive

Maximum decision period is twenty days from electronic filing with the competent body. If not decided in that period, the authorisation is granted by administrative silence. The pending stay or residence is extended until the decision. Decisions are reasoned and may be appealed in alzada.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 76.1

### Forms: MI-T (holder) and MI-F (each family member)

Plataforma ONE: download, complete and sign the holder residence form (MI-T). For family, complete MI-F for each member and file jointly.

**In the file:** Signed MI-T (holder). Signed MI-F for each family member if they file with you.

*Citations:* process:one-dn-residence; process:mi-t; process:mi-f

### Forms: national visa application, signed by the teleworker

The joint instruction and consular pages require the visa application form signed by the teleworker. Use the current national visa form published by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, plus any local copies the competent consulate lists on its page.

**In the file:** National visa application form, completed and signed (copies as the competent consulate currently requires).

*Citations:* process:instruccion-conjunta-teletrabajo; process:national-visa-form

### Fee: modelo 790, code 038, paid before filing, keep the NRC

Plataforma ONE and the official FAQ: pay tasa 038 before starting the electronic file (card, direct debit or Bizum, or bank). Keep the NRC (Número de Referencia Completo) for the submission. The euro amount lives on the official fee page and in the current FAQ — it is a fee tariff, not a statutory income threshold, and it changes. Read the live tasa 038 page on the day you pay.

**In the file:** Proof of payment of tasa 038 and the NRC.

*Citations:* process:one-dn-residence; process:tasa-038; process:dn-faq-es

### Consular visa fee: the post’s current tariff

Art. 62.3.g requires payment of the visa or authorisation fee. The amount is the consular tariff in force at that post, not a figure frozen in this codex. Do not copy euro or dollar amounts from old consular PDFs.

**In the file:** Receipt of the national-visa fee as charged by the competent consulate on the day of filing.

### Complete passport copy

The joint instruction requires a copy of the complete valid passport or travel document (all pages).

**In the file:** Full-colour copy of every passport page, plus the original at the consular appointment.

*Citations:* process:instruccion-conjunta-teletrabajo

### Apostille or legalisation, then sworn translation

Foreign public documents must be apostilled under the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961, or legalised through the Spanish consular/MAE channel, unless an international agreement exempts them. Documents must be translated into Spanish by a sworn translator. Follow the ministry legalisation-and-translation sheet — not blog checklists.

**In the file:** Each foreign public document: apostille or legalisation + sworn Spanish translation, filed behind the source document.

*Citations:* process:legalizacion-traduccion

### TIE if the authorisation lasts more than six months

Once the authorisation is granted, if it has a validity of more than six months, the foreigner must apply for the TIE (tarjeta de identidad de extranjero). File personally at the competent police unit. Use the current official TIE appointment channel.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 76.2; process:tie-cita

### What this authorisation does not permit

An employed holder may not work for companies established in Spain. A professional holder may not let Spanish-sited work exceed 20% of total professional activity. Authorisations and visas under this section will be revoked, refused or not renewed if the foreigner may represent a threat to public order, public security, public health or national security, on the basis of a police, CNI or National Security Department report.

*Citations:* BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 74 bis.1; BOE-A-2013-10074 art. 62.7

## Digital nomad — consular visa packet

File order for the international-telework visa (Ley 14/2013 art. 74 quater).

1. National visa form, signed
2. Consular visa-fee receipt (live tariff)
3. Complete passport copy + original
4. Companies-register certificate (≥ 1 year of activity)
5. Employed: 3-month relationship + remote-work letter
6. Professional: 3-month mercantile relationship + remote terms
7. Evidence the role is exclusively remote
8. Degree / prestige school, or 3 years’ analogous experience
9. Means: 200% SMI formula (contract / offer / funds)
10. Criminal record (2 years) + 5-year sworn declaration
11. Authorised public or private sickness insurance
12. SS coverage certificate or Spanish alta commitment
13. Apostille/legalisation + sworn translations

## Digital nomad — UGE-CE residence packet

File order for the electronic residence if you are already lawfully in Spain (art. 74 quinquies).

1. MI-T signed
2. Tasa 038 paid — keep the NRC
3. Complete passport copy (all pages)
4. Companies-register certificate (≥ 1 year of activity)
5. Employed: 3-month relationship + remote-work letter
6. Professional: 3-month mercantile relationship + remote terms
7. Degree / prestige school, or 3 years’ analogous experience
8. Means: 200% SMI formula
9. Criminal record packet (waived if > 6 months’ Spanish stay/residence — FAQ)
10. Authorised public or private sickness insurance
11. SS coverage or Spanish alta commitment
12. Apostille/legalisation + sworn translations
